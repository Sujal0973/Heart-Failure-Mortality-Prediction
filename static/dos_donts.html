Clinical Protocols & Evidence-Based Lifestyle Guide

Comprehensive clinical management strategies and patient lifestyle recommendations organized across the 5 calibrated risk tiers.

Very Low (<5%)

Stable Cardiac Profile — Maintenance & Preservation

✓ Recommended Actions (Do's)
  • Maintain 100% adherence to prescribed baseline cardiac medications.
  • Engage in 150 minutes of weekly moderate aerobic exercise (walking, swimming).
  • Maintain a balanced Mediterranean-style diet low in processed saturated fats.
  • Schedule routine annual cardiovascular follow-up consultations.
× Precautions (Don'ts)
  • Do not discontinue heart failure medications even if asymptomatic.
  • Avoid high-sodium packaged foods (>2,300 mg daily).
  • Avoid heavy alcohol consumption and tobacco exposure.
Low (5–15%)

Favorable Baseline with Minor Emerging Markers

✓ Recommended Actions (Do's)
  • Restrict daily dietary sodium strictly below 2,000 mg (2 grams) per day.
  • Record daily morning weight upon waking; log in patient symptom journal.
  • Schedule regular outpatient checkups every 6 months with electrolyte review.
  • Stay adequately hydrated without fluid overload.
× Precautions (Don'ts)
  • Do not ignore progressive pedal swelling or unusual ankle puffiness.
  • Avoid over-the-counter NSAIDs (e.g., ibuprofen, naproxen) which induce renal fluid retention.
  • Do not engage in unaccustomed intense isometric strain without physician clearance.
Moderate (15–35%)

Elevated Physiological Stress — Comprehensive Review

⚠️ Clinical Management Protocols
  • Book a dedicated clinical cardiology review within 2 to 4 weeks.
  • Order follow-up comprehensive metabolic panel: serum creatinine, BUN, and sodium/potassium.
  • Evaluate guideline-directed medical therapy (GDMT) optimization: ACEi/ARNI, beta-blocker, SGLT2i, MRA.
  • Maintain daily fluid restriction (typically 1.5–2.0 liters/day if advised by physician).
× Critical Red Flags to Watch
  • Watch for weight gain exceeding 1.5 kg (3.3 lbs) over 48 hours.
  • Do not overlook shortness of breath upon lying flat (orthopnea requiring extra pillows).
  • Avoid salt substitutes containing high potassium unless specifically approved by nephrologist.
High (35–70%)

Significant Decompensation Risk — Urgent Consultation

🚨 Priority Clinical Interventions
  • Contact cardiology specialist clinic for prompt priority consultation within 48 to 72 hours.
  • Evaluate need for intravenous or escalated loop diuretic dosing for congestion relief.
  • Perform echocardiogram to re-evaluate left ventricular ejection fraction and valve competence.
  • Assess cardio-renal status; monitor for acute kidney injury exacerbation.
× Immediate Warning Thresholds
  • Rapid fluid accumulation (>2 kg / 4.4 lbs in 2 days) requires immediate clinic notification.
  • Do not delay seeking acute care for chest pressure, persistent cough, or daytime confusion.
  • Rest in an upright or elevated head-of-bed posture.
Very High (≥70%)

Critical Mortality Probability — Emergency Care Required

🚑 Emergency Action Protocol
  • Seek immediate emergency department or acute heart failure inpatient triage.
  • Bring all current prescription bottles, recent laboratory panels, and this assessment report.
  • Prepare for continuous hemodynamic monitoring, intravenous inotropic support, or intensive telemetry.
  • Conduct multi-disciplinary review with advanced heart failure and heart transplantation teams if indicated.
× Life-Threatening Manifestations
  • Severe dyspnea at rest, pink frothy sputum (pulmonary edema), or cyanosis.
  • Syncope (fainting), profound lethargy, cold extremities, or systolic BP < 90 mmHg.
  • Do not drive oneself to the hospital; summon emergency medical transport immediately.
